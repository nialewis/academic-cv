summary: Seminar presenting two STAR measurements testing whether baryon number is carried by the valence quarks or by a Y-shaped gluon "baryon junction," using isobar collision and photonuclear collision data.
abstract: |
  Baryon number is a strictly conserved quantum number, conventionally assumed to be split equally among a baryon's three valence quarks, though this has never been directly verified. An alternative model, the baryon junction, proposes instead a Y-shaped configuration of nonperturbative gluons connected to all three valence quarks that carries the baryon number. This talk presents two STAR measurements bearing on which picture is correct. The first compares baryon stopping to electric charge stopping over large momentum transfer using identified particle spectra from Ru+Ru and Zr+Zr isobar collisions: since valence quarks carry the baryon's electric charge, if they also carried the baryon number these two quantities should match, but the data show midrapidity baryon stopping is roughly twice as large as charge stopping in central collisions. The second measurement looks at photonuclear collisions — a type of ultraperipheral heavy-ion collision where one nucleus emits a quasi-real photon that interacts with the other colliding nucleus — where significant baryon stopping and rapidity asymmetry are observed at low transverse momentum in Au+Au collisions, further supporting a baryon number carrier distinct from the valence quarks.
